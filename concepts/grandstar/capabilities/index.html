Injection moulding

34 presses from 50 to 850 tonnes. Six run inside an ISO class 8 clean cell for medical and optical parts, and two have a rotary platen for two-shot moulding.

Every press above 180 tonnes has a robot, so cycle time and part handling stay the same from the first shot to the last.

Mould making

A mould is two blocks of steel with your part missing from the middle. Select a region to see what it does and why it matters to your part.

Mould classes we build

  • SPI 104

    SteelAluminium 7075 or P20

    Guaranteed lifeUp to 100,000 shots

    Typical usePrototype and bridge tools

  • SPI 103

    SteelP20, 718H

    Guaranteed lifeUp to 500,000 shots

    Typical useMost consumer and industrial parts

  • SPI 102

    SteelH13, NAK80

    Guaranteed lifeUp to 1,000,000 shots

    Typical useGlass-filled resins, high-gloss surfaces

  • SPI 101

    SteelH13, S136 hardened to 48–52 HRC

    Guaranteed lifeOver 1,000,000 shots

    Typical useMedical, packaging, multi-cavity

Cavity and core steel is held to ±0.02 mm. Tools can be built to DME, Hasco or LKM standards, and to your own tooling specification if you have one.

3D printing

Six industrial printers. Most of their work is bridge production: a few hundred printed parts that keep your launch on schedule while the mould is being cut.
