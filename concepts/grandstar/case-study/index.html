Case study · E-mobility · 2025

A glass-filled lid that would not lie flat.

A controller lid has to seal to IP67. This one bowed by 0.61 mm across its seal face, and one part in sixteen failed the leak test. Six weeks after the tool reached us it measured 0.18 mm.

The job

Customer
E-bike drive maker, Bavaria
Part
Motor controller lid
Size
186 × 94 × 22 mm
Material
PA66 GF30, black
Tool
2-cavity H13, 250 t press
Volume
9,000 parts a month

The bow, measured

Each bar is the height of the seal face above its datum plane at one of nine stations along the lid. Anything above the dashed line leaks.

Peak deviation
0.61 mm
Stations over the limit
5 of 9

Six weeks, two trials

The first rework fixed the flatness and created a cosmetic fault. We include it because that is how tool development usually goes.

  1. 1

    3 March 2025

    Tool arrives

    The mould was transferred from the customer’s previous supplier. We ran 30 shots at their settings and scanned every part to fix a baseline.

  2. 2

    7 March

    Cause found

    Fill simulation matched the scans. A single gate on the short edge was laying the glass fibres along the lid, so it shrank less lengthwise than across and bowed.

  3. 3

    12 March

    Proposal signed off

    Move the gate to the centre through a short hot drop, add two ribs across the lid, and replace the steel core insert with a copper-alloy one on its own cooling circuit.

  4. 4

    26 March

    First trial after rework

    Flatness came in at 0.22 mm, inside the limit. The ribs, at 6 mm tall, left faint sink lines on the outer face.

  5. 5

    2 April

    Second trial

    Ribs cut down to 4 mm, which removed the sink. A sixteen-run study on packing pressure and mould temperature settled the process at 0.18 mm.

  6. 6

    14 April

    Production released

    PPAP level 3 approved by the customer. The first 9,000 parts shipped by sea on 28 April.
